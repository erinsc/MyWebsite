Magnetics

A zero-mass computing medium concept, with an example use-case for a powerful yet limited and costly FTL travel

The idea started relatively innocuous. Computer models suggested that by creating a precise shape of a magnetic field in space, it could support itself for a while without any need for conductors or other structures. It could be used for all sorts of applications, like magnetic gears with low resistance, or maybe more sensitive touchscreens and other detectors.

The real breakthrough came through however when one person at the Electron United labs noticed, that when they turned off their "Knitting machine", the magnetic field-lines didn't seem to collapse uniformly as expected, and instead kind of, swung around, not unlike a string unravelling. Indeed when she modelled some patterns in a simulation, there was a lot more going on inside than they previously thought. After a few days of thinking, she eventually came up with a way to control the untangling how she wanted, and successfully made the first logic gate made out of not a single atom.

Obviously it was far from perfect. For one it was one-time use only, and it required a few Kilowatts of energy to construct, but that was an engineering problem, not a theoretical one. In a week's time the lab team was able to build a template for an 11-bit adder that could work reliably. Only reason they didn't extend the adder longer was because of the limited size of their knitter, so obviously they requested more funds to construct a more precise coil array, and materials that should play nicer with the mechanisms.

There wasn't any reason to make the device bigger. If anything, the smaller the knit the faster it would unravel, the faster the calculation would run, not that it mattered right now, but the plan was already to build a circuit large and complicated enough to prove economical to use in real work.

2 Years of r&d and a few sleepless nights, "Maggie" was advanced enough to build and then collapse a magnetic circuit large enough to beat the office computers at some tasks, especially on numeric and very parallelised problems, a highlight was a Mersenne prime number checker, which was able to check numbers up to 2^2^32 - 1 on a good day, actually beating the prime number record of the day! although the result had to be checked on a classical computer. The size of the knit kept shrinking while its equivalent transistor density kept increasing, the computing capacity kept doubling very roughly every 8 days or so.

Unlike with silicon-based computers, there wasn't really anything that was limiting the size of the circuitry, since no mass meant no quantum tunnelling, no uncertainty principle, nothing. The entropy of any given variator - the name given to the equivalent of a transistor - of course shrank with its size, but as long as there were enough amplifiers the signal could be recovered without major issue. Some of the smallest variators were now measured in picometers.

The ISV Sarajevo engaged its manoeuvring thrusters to reach higher orbit above earth. After 3 months in dock it was finally loaded with all 200 petagrams of precious cargo that will be used for colonisation of Kepler-22b, from precious metals and resources, to complex machinery and all 5000 crew suspended in stasis. Under constant thrust, the journey to the system will only take a few years, but the main issue is that it's basically impossible to evade any stars or planets in the way, but also any gravity wells caused by nearby objects. even a small planetoid could potentially veer the ship yotameters off course with no way to correct the path. It is vitally important that the entire space from Earth to Kepler is carefully and precisely mapped out town to the last object, only made harder by the fact that astronomical data was necessarily out of date; the further a star was the longer its light took to get here.

In the heart of the ship, between two capacitor banks stretching 4 floors down and surrounded by a jungle of cooling pipes and power relays, not enough space between the walls for a person to fit through, was a polished metal cylinder barely a meter wide, inside it a carefully controlled vacuum surrounded two bronze metal plates, pointing towards each other as if outlining the space between them. Except a dull hum of the walls, there was no evidence that the chamber suddenly filled with a surge of energy. The two bronze plates were not smooth, but lined with uncountable array of coils, too small to see, now switching between each other strands of magnetic field-lines, as if in a stellar dance exchanging, splitting and merging them over and over, first slowly to form big strands, then faster and smaller, the slowly formed the basic circuitry that would eventually perform the necessary calculations and propel the ship to its destination.

As the invisible strings tangled and tightened to the size of protons and neutrons, gaining in strength and intensity to almost impossible levels, the surrounding machinery only cooled the surrounding space to freezing temperatures. Any living creature here would surely perish, if not from the cold then from the little leaking magnetism that would strangle its brain. Just outside the affected area the space is quiet. The ship slowly moves to its starting position, and angles its main fusion thrusters roughly to the antipode of kepler's future position in the sky, prepaired for the final navigational information.

If anyone on the ship was awake, they would hear a sudden thud permeating through the ship, and all the metallic walls trill with a low hum as suddenly an enormous amount of energy poured from deep within. The intricate magnetic mechanism began untangling with incredible speed, its uncountable threads violently lashing around each other, faster than electrons could move in a wire, with enough energy to level a small building. Propagating through to the smallest and most complicated portions of the matrix, trillions of bits of information exchanged hands and simulated the movements of entire systems with accuracy down to a few meters, refining and improving its own estimates in a pseudo-recursive pattern. Half the galaxy simulated in the volume of a baseball, whizzing around its digital universe. Three or so seconds after the computation had started, the final results were already pouring in, and with another loud thud all the remaining energy dissipated into the environment, its surrounding room now red hot, just as inhospitable as it started.

The main engines engage while manoeuvring thrusters angled the ship by 5 degrees 32 minutes yaw, 52 minutes pitch to correct its course, and keep making small adjustments as the ship leaves orbit. In a few hours the Sarajevo is a small blue streak in the distance, adjusting its trajectory while passing mars on its orbit.
